# End Credits — Visual Direction

## North star

End Credits is a personal cinema-culture platform: dark, immersive, youthful, editorial, and a little playful. It should feel made for people who remember how a film made them feel—not like a corporate dashboard, a streaming-service clone, or a generic app with cinematic decoration.

## Core language

- **Atmosphere:** near-black blue backgrounds, icy-blue illumination, off-white type, photographic grain, and one considered accent color drawn from imagery when useful.
- **Typography:** oversized, condensed display type is a primary visual object, not simply a heading. Pair it with quiet, highly legible utility text for controls and metadata.
- **Composition:** use the viewport like a title frame—editorial asymmetry, decisive negative space, cropped monumental words, image/type overlap, and functional elements placed with intention rather than surrounded by cards.
- **Cinema cues:** use original film stills, film frames, viewfinder corners, timecodes, rolling-credit metadata, subtitles, and frame counters as structural details—not as scattered icons.

## Interaction and motion

- Keep motion slow, subtle, and cinematic: text/still reveals, an occasional scan line, and precise control feedback.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Keep actions obvious. The cinematic layer must not reduce contrast, readability, focus visibility, or form usability.

## Reuse rules

- Prefer the existing near-black palette, icy-blue signature accent, display/body type split, fine rules, and metadata labels before introducing a new visual treatment.
- Use an accent to direct attention, not to color every component. Avoid neon, gaming/cyberpunk treatments, and ornamental glow.
- Let original stills, poster art, and film metadata become the visual focus on future movie pages; product chrome should remain quiet and distinctive.
- Avoid rounded-card SaaS patterns, dense card grids, heavy gradients, excessive glass, generic dashboard widgets, and layouts that resemble Netflix.

## Responsive intent

On small screens, retain the oversized editorial statement and one strong cinema object, then prioritize a single-column, easy-to-complete task flow. Decoration may reduce; identity should not.

## Authentication exception

Authentication is deliberately quieter than the future cinema archive. It is a centered, oversized premium admission ticket: a printed main pass for brand and access plus a dark tear-off stub. Use one clear task, restrained serial metadata, a perforated divider with inward notch cuts, barcode detail, and contrasting type scales. Do not add posters, dashboards, or a logged-in home experience to this screen. The logged-in visual world will be designed as a separate phase.
